What it does

Everything a parent actually asks for.

At a glance

Know what’s happening, right now

Open the app and see which computers are on and who is at the screen, on every computer in the house.

  • Who is logged in, and since when
  • Which apps they have open, and the websites and searches they open, within seconds
  • A notification the moment someone logs in, or searches for something bad
  • Screenshots of a child’s screen, to swipe through like your gallery and save to your phone
  • Still there when a computer is off: change its rules and it gets them the moment it is back

Time

Bedtime that means bedtime

Set the hours once. A warning comes first, then the account logs out or the computer shuts down.

  • Daily screen-time limits, with extra time in one tap
  • Weekly bedtime and open hours
  • A week of each child’s time as a bar chart, and every app with its time

Apps and websites

Block what shouldn’t be there

Choose the apps and websites an account may not use. Blocked apps close within seconds and you are told. Your child can be shown why, if you turn notices on.

  • One switch for Minecraft (any launcher), Discord, Roblox or Steam; press and hold any other app
  • Allow only the sites you pick and block the rest of the web — a VPN cannot get around it
  • Adult filter and safe search, and an AI filter for ChatGPT, Gemini and 90 more, in school hours if you like
  • Be told the moment a bad word is typed, even without pressing enter, or spelled in a tricky way or backwards
  • Only installed programs: nothing downloaded or from a USB drive runs
  • New children start protected, with no setting up

Control

Stay in charge, even of the password

Log your child in from your phone without saying the password out loud. Or make the account open only when you allow it.

  • Log in from the phone: the login screen opens their account by itself
  • Forgot a password? See it on your phone, behind your fingerprint
  • Internet off for one account, while the computer stays on
  • You’re told if someone tries to cut the network

Install

Pick your computer.

  1. From your administrator account, download CurfewSetup.exe and open it.
  2. If Windows warns about an unknown publisher, choose More info, then Run anyway.
  3. Type each child's password once, then scan the code with your phone.

From your administrator account, open a terminal and paste this:

curl -fsSL https://sharjeelmazhar.github.io/Curfew/install.sh | sudo sh

Then connect your phone:

sudo curfew pair

Works on Ubuntu 20.04 and newer, Debian 12 and 13, Fedora and Arch. New versions arrive with the computer's own updates; on Arch, run the first command again.

  1. Download Curfew.apk on your phone and open it.
  2. Allow installing from this source when Android asks. If Play Protect does not recognise it, choose More details, then Install anyway.
  3. Tap Add computer and scan the code.

Your family's data

Built so that only you hold the key.

Signed by your phone

Only a phone that scanned the code can control the computer. Every message is signed with a key made at pairing.

Works away from home

Away from the Wi-Fi, messages pass through a small relay, sealed with that same key, so the relay can't read them.

Your child can't remove it

With a standard account they can't stop, change or remove Curfew, or read its keys.

About Curfew

Small, fast and
built to be hard to break.

Curfew is written in two modern languages, each chosen for the job it does. Nothing is wrapped in a browser, and nothing extra has to be installed.

Rust

On the computer

The program on each computer is written in Rust, which is known for its speed and its memory safety. The language rejects whole families of memory bugs, such as buffer overflows and use-after-free, before a program can even be built.

Kotlin

On your phone

The app is a native Android app, written in Kotlin with Jetpack Compose. Native means quick to open and smooth to use, and Kotlin's type system guards against the crashes that null values cause.

Questions

The honest answers.

Can a child get around it?

Not from a standard account. With physical access a determined child can do more: start from a USB stick, use recovery mode, flip a Wi-Fi switch, or learn your administrator password. Curfew is a strong remote control, not a safe. The full list is here.

Do I need Tailscale or a VPN to use it away from home?

No. Once your phone has reached a computer at home, it reaches it from anywhere, with nothing to set up. Messages pass through a small relay, sealed with your phone's key, so the relay can't read them. If you already use Tailscale, it keeps working too.

Can it see private or incognito windows?

No, they leave nothing behind. You can turn private windows off for an account instead.

Can I see my child's screen?

Yes, as screenshots, and only of a child's account, never an administrator's. Take one when you ask, or have them taken every 15 seconds, or every 1, 5 or 15 minutes — and, if you like, only while an app such as Discord or Minecraft is open. Your child is not told and sees nothing when one is taken. Screenshots stay on the computer for 7 days and on your phone, encrypted, up to the size you choose. They travel sealed with your phone's key.

Can they watch YouTube during an online class?

Not when the account is set for class. The browsers open only the class sites, and YouTube and TikTok are closed in every program at the network, including the YouTube tab inside Microsoft Teams.

Can they turn the speaker down or off?

No. A child cannot switch Bluetooth off, disconnect the speaker you paired or pair another device. The speaker and every program stay at half volume or louder; if they turn it down, it goes straight back up and you are told.

Is there an iPhone app?

Not yet. The app is Android only, and the computers can be Windows or Linux. There's no Mac version yet.

Do I need to create an account?

No. You scan a code once, and that's it.

Does it slow the computer down?

No. It's one small program that uses very little memory and spends most of its time idle.
